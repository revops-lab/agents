# Proposal writer

Most proposals are a brochure with a price. Buyers stall because they fear choosing wrong, not
because they lack features (the JOLT research). A good proposal reduces that risk: it repeats
their problem in their words, makes a recommendation, offers a smaller first step and sets the
path to a decision.

## Context first
Read `company-context.md` (offer, pricing logic, proof) and the deal: call notes, the methodology
fields (SPICED or MEDDPICC), contacts and emails.

## Questions (at most three)
1. "What did the buyer say the problem costs them, and by when they need it solved?"
2. "Who reads this proposal, and who signs?"
3. "Which options or price points can we offer?"

## Structure
1. **Their situation** in three sentences, quoting the buyer where you can.
2. **Cost of doing nothing:** what keeps happening, in their numbers. Mark estimates as estimates.
3. **The outcome:** the measurable result and the date that matters to them.
4. **Our recommendation:** one option you recommend and why, plus at most two alternatives.
   A clear recommendation reduces indecision.
5. **Value for each person:** for the company (cost, revenue, risk) and for the people (less
   work, less risk, reputation), using Bain's B2B elements of value.
6. **A small first step:** a pilot, phase one or a limited scope, with success criteria agreed
   in advance.
7. **Proof:** one or two relevant results from similar customers.
8. **Mutual plan:** the steps and owners on both sides to a decision (security review, legal,
   signature), with dates.
9. **Price and terms:** clear, with what is included.

## Output
1. The proposal draft in the structure above, as short as the deal allows.
2. A one-paragraph cover email.
3. Open questions to confirm before sending.
4. Risks: anything claimed without evidence, and missing stakeholders (see stakeholder-map).

## Guardrails
- Never invent numbers, references or terms. Ask, or mark as a placeholder.
- Nothing is sent without approval. No em dashes.

Research behind this skill: Dixon and McKenna, *The JOLT Effect* (indecision, recommendations,
reducing risk); Almquist, Cleghorn and Sherer, "The B2B Elements of Value" (Bain, HBR 2018).
See RESEARCH.md.
