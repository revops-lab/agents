# Closed-lost revival

Many lost deals were not lost to a competitor. They ended in no decision, or "not now". When the
reason was timing, budget or a missing stakeholder, the deal often comes back when something
changes at the company. Snitcher describes the pattern: after months of silence, a burst of
pricing or comparison page visits from a company that said "not now".

## Context first
Read `company-context.md` and closed-lost deals from the last 6 to 24 months with their reasons,
notes and contacts.

## Questions
1. "How far back should we look, and which deal size is worth reopening?"
2. "Do you track website visits by company? (CRM, a visitor tool, or none)"

## Step 1: win-loss analysis
Group lost deals by the real reason, not just the dropdown: no decision (fear, no urgency),
budget, timing, a competitor, missing feature, champion left, poor fit. Compare with won deals of
the same size. Quote buyers where notes allow. Report the top three reasons with counts and what
the team could change (process, proof, offer).

## Step 2: who is worth reopening
Keep deals lost for timing, budget, no decision or champion left, and drop poor fit and lost to a
competitor within the last 12 months unless a signal suggests otherwise. Then look for a new
signal since the loss: new leader in the buying function, hiring for the role your product
supports, funding, pricing or comparison page visits, a reply or meeting, the competitor's
contract renewal date if known.

## Step 3: the reason to reconnect
One short note per account that names what changed, refers honestly to the earlier conversation
and offers something useful (a new result, a smaller first step). Never pretend the earlier no
did not happen.

## If you use HubSpot
Read closed-lost deals: Deal stage (`dealstage`), Closed lost reason (`closed_lost_reason`),
Close date (`closedate`), Amount (`amount`), and associated contacts and companies. For website
activity read the company or contact Last visit (`hs_analytics_last_visit_timestamp`) and Page
views (`hs_analytics_num_page_views`) where available. Propose new deals or tasks; create them
only after approval. Confirm internal names with the connector's property search.

## Output
1. Win-loss summary: top reasons with counts, quotes and one change each.
2. Shortlist to reopen: account, lost reason, what changed, score.
3. A draft note per account.
4. Proposed tasks or deals.

## Guardrails
- Base every "what changed" on a dated source.
- Nothing is sent or created without approval. No em dashes.

Research behind this skill: Dixon and McKenna, *The JOLT Effect* (no decision); Snitcher,
"Your lost deals are coming back" (October 2026); Kyle Poyar, Growth Unhinged (September 2026).
