# Sales battlecard

A battlecard is only useful if a seller can use it in the middle of a call. One page, one
competitor, built from what buyers actually said, not from the competitor's website.

## Context first
Read `company-context.md` (alternatives, positioning, proof). Then read deals where this
competitor appeared: won, lost and open, with their reasons and call notes.

## Questions (at most two)
1. "Which competitor, and in which segment do you meet them most?"
2. "Do you have notes from deals you lost to them?"

## Method
- **Start from the buyer's alternatives** (April Dunford): the competitor, doing nothing, a
  spreadsheet, building it in-house. Often "do nothing" wins more deals than any competitor.
- **Evidence first.** Separate what buyers said (quote, deal, date) from what the competitor
  claims publicly. Mark public claims as claims.
- **Where each side is strong.** Write their real strengths honestly; sellers lose trust in
  battlecards that pretend the competitor is weak.
- **Landmine questions.** Fair questions that expose a gap if it matters to this buyer, for
  example about setup time, a missing integration or total cost. Never false or disparaging.
- **Objection answers** in three parts: acknowledge, reframe with evidence, ask a question.

## Output (one page)
1. When you meet them: segment, typical buyer, deal size.
2. Why buyers pick them (honest) and why buyers pick you, with deal evidence.
3. Three landmine questions.
4. Top five objections with answers.
5. Proof: two customer results against this competitor.
6. Pricing and packaging notes (public sources, dated).
7. What not to say.
8. Sources and date of last update.

## If you use HubSpot
Read closed deals with Closed won reason (`closed_won_reason`) and Closed lost reason
(`closed_lost_reason`), and any competitor property your team uses (confirm with the connector's
property search). Suggest a competitor field if none exists; create it only after approval.

## Guardrails
- Never state anything about a competitor that you cannot source. Date every public fact.
- No disparaging language. No em dashes.

Research behind this skill: April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (alternatives); Dixon and McKenna,
*The JOLT Effect* ("no decision" as the main competitor). See RESEARCH.md.
