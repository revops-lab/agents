# Content brief

Good B2B content answers a question a buyer actually has, better than anything else on the first
page, and states the answer early enough that a search engine or an AI assistant can quote it.
You decide what to write from demand and evidence, then give the writer everything they need.

## Context first
Read `company-context.md`: buyer words, buying situations, proof, what not to say.

## Questions
1. "Do you have a topic, or should I find the best opportunity?"
2. "Which data can I use?" (Google Ads Keyword Planner, Search Console, a keyword file, none)
3. "Who writes it, and how long can it be?"

## Step 1: choose the target
- With Keyword Planner: monthly searches, competition and the 12-month trend for the topic and
  close variants. With Search Console: queries where the site already shows on page 2 or has
  impressions but few clicks (fastest wins).
- Prefer terms that match a buying situation over pure definitions.
- Say the intent behind the term: learn, compare, or buy. One page serves one intent.

## Step 2: what already ranks
Read the top results and any AI overview for the term. Note what they all cover (table stakes),
what they miss, and what is out of date. The page must add something real: data, a worked
example, a template, a clearer answer.

## Step 3: the outline (answer first)
- **Title** with the main term, under 60 characters.
- **The answer in the first two sentences**, self-contained.
- Sections built from the questions buyers ask (from search suggestions, forums, sales calls).
  Each section starts with its own short answer.
- **Evidence:** cite sources, add statistics with their date and source, and quotations from
  named experts. Research on generative engines (Aggarwal et al., 2023) found these increase the
  chance of being cited.
- A worked example, template or checklist the reader can use today.
- FAQ with three to five real questions, answered in two sentences each.
- Internal links to and from related pages; one clear next step.

## Step 4: make it findable
Meta description (about 150 characters, answer first), suggested structured data (Article, FAQ,
HowTo where true), image alt text, and the internal pages that should link to it.

## Output
The brief: target and intent with numbers, what ranks and the gap, outline with headings and the
answer line for each, sources and statistics to use, FAQ, links, metadata. Mark any statistic you
could not verify.

## Guardrails
- Never invent statistics or quotes. Mark "needs source" instead.
- Do not copy competitor text.
- No em dashes.

Research behind this skill: Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (2023);
Ehrenberg-Bass on buying situations. See RESEARCH.md.
