# Account plan

Retention compounds: Bain's work with Frederick Reichheld showed small gains in retention have an
outsized effect on profit. An account plan makes growth and retention deliberate for the few
customers who matter most.

## Context first
Read `company-context.md` and the account: deals, contacts, usage or support notes, past QBRs and
renewal date.

## Questions (at most three)
1. "What are this customer's top goals this year, in their words?"
2. "Which teams or regions do not use you yet?"
3. "Who is your champion now, and who signs the renewal?"

## Sections
1. **Their goals** and how you support each (Bain's B2B elements of value: business and personal).
2. **Value so far:** results with numbers and dates, and what the customer would say about you.
3. **Relationship map:** people, roles, stance and last contact (see stakeholder-map). Flag
   single-threaded relationships and the renewal signer.
4. **Health and risks:** usage, support, sentiment, champion changes, budget changes (see
   renewal-risk-check).
5. **Growth:** new teams, use cases or regions, each with a reason the customer would care.
6. **90-day plan:** at most five actions with owners and dates, on both sides.

## If you use HubSpot
Read the company: Number of associated deals (`num_associated_deals`), Open deals
(`hs_num_open_deals`), Recent deal close date (`recent_deal_close_date`), Total revenue
(`total_revenue`), and contacts with Buying role (`hs_buying_role`) and Last contacted
(`notes_last_contacted`). Propose tasks for the 90-day plan; create them only after approval.
Confirm internal names with the connector's property search.

## Output
One page in the sections above, plus open questions and proposed CRM tasks.

## Guardrails
- Never invent results; mark unknowns.
- Nothing is written to the CRM without approval. No em dashes.

Research behind this skill: Frederick Reichheld, Bain & Company (retention economics);
Almquist, Cleghorn and Sherer, "The B2B Elements of Value" (HBR 2018). See RESEARCH.md.
