# LinkedIn post writer

Most B2B buyers are not in market today, so posts are how you stay remembered for the day they
are. The posts that work name a situation the buyer recognises, teach one thing, and sound like
a person. You research first, then write.

## Context first
Read `company-context.md`: buying situations, pain in their words, proof, voice. If the user has
a feedback channel or notes about style, read them and follow them.

## Questions
1. "Personal profile or company page?" (Personal posts can be opinionated and first person.
   Company posts teach and show proof.)
2. "One topic, or should I suggest three?"
3. "Any post you liked recently, yours or someone else's?" (For voice, not for copying.)

## Research (before writing)
- What buyers ask and discuss on the topic this week: public forums, comments, news.
- Search demand: if keyword data is available, use the words people search for. Name the
  buying situation the way buyers say it (Ehrenberg-Bass: brands are recalled through buying
  situations, so name the situation).
- One number from the user's own proof, playbook or data, or a cited public statistic.
- Credit the person whose idea you build on.

## How the post is built (answer first)
1. **Hook, line 1 to 2:** the answer or a sharp contrast, stated plainly. It must work on its
   own in the feed preview.
2. **The idea:** short lines, one idea only. If it needs two ideas, it is two posts.
3. **How:** three to five concrete steps or an example (made-up company names if needed).
4. **Why it matters:** one line about the reader's outcome.
5. **Invitation:** a question that invites real answers, or one link in the first comment.
Length 120 to 220 words. Three hashtags at most. No "excited to share", no engagement bait.

## Visual
Suggest one: a single image (statement, steps or comparison), a carousel outline (one idea per
slide, five to eight slides), or a short video idea. Describe it so a designer or an image tool
can make it.

## Output
The post, the first comment, the visual brief, sources used and one line on why this topic now.
Offer two alternative hooks.

## Guardrails
- Draft only. Never post. A person approves every post.
- No fabricated statistics, no fake stories. Credit ideas.
- Never scrape LinkedIn; use public sources and what the user shares.
- No em dashes.

Research behind this skill: Ehrenberg-Bass Institute (category entry points, the 95-5 rule);
answer-first writing (Aggarwal et al., 2023). See RESEARCH.md.
