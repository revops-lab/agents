# Objection helper

Most objections are not about the thing said. "Too expensive" often means "I am not sure this
will work and I do not want to be blamed". Research on 2.5 million sales calls (Dixon and
McKenna, *The JOLT Effect*) found most lost deals ended in no decision, and most of those came
from indecision, not loyalty to the old way. So you diagnose before you answer.

## Context first
Read `company-context.md` (proof, value by level, alternatives). If there are call notes or a
deal record, read them.

## Questions
1. "What exactly did they say, word for word if you can?"
2. "Who said it, and who else is involved in the decision?"
3. "What has happened in the deal so far?" (stage, last meeting, what they agreed to)

## Diagnose: which force is it?
Use the forces of progress. A switch happens when push (problem now) plus pull (better future)
outweigh anxiety (fear of the new) plus habit (the current way).
- **Weak push:** the problem is not painful or urgent enough. Signs: "not a priority", "maybe
  next year". Move: go back to impact and the critical event; quantify the cost of waiting.
- **Weak pull:** they do not see how you are better than the alternative. Signs: "how are you
  different from X?" Move: differentiated proof against their real alternative.
- **Anxiety (indecision):** fear of picking wrong, of the project failing, of looking bad.
  Signs: "we need more information", endless questions, more people added late. Move (from the
  JOLT research): give a clear recommendation instead of more options, limit the scope of the
  first step, take risk off the table (pilot, success criteria, exit clause).
- **Habit:** the old way is easy and familiar. Signs: "we will manage with spreadsheets". Move:
  make the first step tiny and show what stays the same.
- **Missing person:** the person objecting is not the one who decides. Move: ask to involve the
  decision maker, with a reason that helps the champion.

## Draft the response
1. **Acknowledge and ask first.** One open question that confirms the real concern
   ("When you say timing, what would need to be true for this to be a priority now?").
2. **Answer the real concern** with one piece of evidence from the context file.
3. **Propose the next step** that reduces risk for them.
Give a version for a call (spoken, short) and for an email (under 120 words).

## Proposals (when asked)
Structure: their situation and goals in their words; the cost of doing nothing; the recommended
option (one, not three equal ones); what happens in the first 30 days; success criteria; price
and terms; risks and how they are handled. Recommend, do not overwhelm: buyers who are helped to
make sense of options buy more confidently (Gartner buyer enablement research).

## Output
Diagnosis (which force, with evidence), the question to ask, the answer, the next step, and
what to log in the CRM.

## Guardrails
- Never invent proof, discounts or terms the user did not approve.
- No manipulation: no false urgency, no pressure tactics.
- No em dashes.

Research behind this skill: Dixon and McKenna, *The JOLT Effect* (2022); Bob Moesta, forces of
progress; Gartner buyer enablement. See RESEARCH.md.
